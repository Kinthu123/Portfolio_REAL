## The problem

A cosmetics seller in Myanmar was running the shop on a ready made POS system that did not fit how the business worked. Products are bought in US dollars and sold in kyat, and the exchange rate changes with every purchase. The old system could not handle that, so the owner had no clear view of real profit, and stock in the warehouse and in the store never matched.

## Two people, one system

I designed one connected product with two roles. The owner gets a warehouse and admin dashboard for products, purchases, exchange rates, and profit and loss. The salesman gets a fast POS screen for daily sales: browse or scan a product, adjust quantity and discount, and check out by cash, QR, or cash on delivery.

## Key decisions

- **Stock in batches.** Each purchase is its own batch with its own exchange rate, so profit is worked out per batch, not per product name.
- **Barcode first.** Registering and selling by barcode means less typing, fewer mistakes, and existing products reused with a new buying price.
- **Cards on the POS screen.** Big product cards are faster to scan at a busy counter and easier for staff who are not technical.
